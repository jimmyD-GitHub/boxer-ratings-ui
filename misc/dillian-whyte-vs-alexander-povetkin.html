Alexander Povetkin

12 Round WBC Interim Heavyweight Title Fight

Matchroom Fight Camp, Brentwood, Essex - August 22nd 2020

Dillian Whyte ( 27-1 ) will be risking his mandatory contender position with the WBC when he takes on the ageing but always dangerous Alexander Povetkin ( 35-2-1 ) in the final edition of Eddie Hearn's "Matchroom Fight Camp" series.

It is not an understatement to suggest that defeat would be a disaster for Whyte, who has campaigned long and hard to secure a shot at the WBC Heavyweight title held by Tyson Fury. Losing to Povetkin could mean falling at the final hurdle.

As things stand Fury is apparently obligated to defend against the winner of this bout in February 2021, although that seems unlikely given that he is scheduled to face Deontay Wilder for a third time in Las Vegas before the end of 2020.

Much has been made of the fact that Whyte has held the WBC's #1 contender position for more than a year without being given the opportunity to box for the title. The London fighter's detractors are quick to point out that Whyte has rejected more than one offer to contest a rematch with reigning WBA, IBF and WBO Heavyweight champion Anthony Joshua during that time. The 32-year-old's only career loss came when he was KO'd by Joshua in December 2015.

Whyte polarises opinion amongst boxing fans like few other currently active fighters. Depending on who you speak to he is either grossly overrated or highly underrated. Personally I feel that he is a legitimate top 10 contender with obvious strengths and weaknesses.

Whyte is most comfortable boxing from distance behind a strong orthodox left jab. He uses body shots to set his opponent up for a big left hook. That shot is easily his most potent weapon. In the best performance of his career, the Brixton man dropped former WBO Heavyweight champion Joseph Parker heavily with a left hook in round nine on his way to a unanimous decision victory.

Whyte is physically rugged, tough and durable but not too elusive. His foot work and movement can appear awkward at times and his balance is questionable. He has a bad habit of stumbling when he moves backwards, something that was noticeable now and then during his unanimous decision win over Oscar Rivas in July 2019. Whyte has KO power in the left hand but his right is underwhelming by world level standards.

Those limitations would prevent me from backing Whyte to win any bout against Fury, Joshua or Wilder, but I believe he can box on equal terms with anyone else in the division, with the possible exception of Oleksandr Usyk.

In times gone by Povetkin would have been the betting favourite to defeat Whyte, but at the age of 40 there are big question marks over what the former WBA Heavyweight champion still has to offer. In his most recent performances, the Russian looked laboured and slow when going 12 rounds with Hughie Fury and Michael Hunter. Many observers felt that Povetkin was lucky to escape with a split decision draw against Hunter in December 2019.

At his best Povetkin could look devastating, as he did when taking out former world champion Hasim Rahman inside two rounds back in 2012. As recently as March 2018 he showed himself still capable of dispatching much bigger men by knocking out David Price in round five.

The Price victory earned Povetkin a shot at Anthony Joshua's world titles. Povetkin gave a good account of himself despite losing by TKO in the 7th round but since that bout in September 2018 the magic appears to have gone. Like other smaller heavyweights with an explosive style, Povetkin has found it increasingly difficult to maintain his effectiveness in the latter stages of his career.

Povetkin is likely to start fast and will be at his most dangerous in the early rounds, particuarly with left hooks and right uppercuts on the inside. The Russian will also jab to the body as a way of creating opportunities to land his powerful overhand right. Whyte may have to come through some difficult moments but once the pace slows, he should be able to control Povetkin from long range behind his jab. I don't believe Povetkin has the stamina to sustain the intensity of his attacks beyond 4 rounds.

Povetkin has only failed to go the distance with Anthony Joshua during his professional career. This could become a punishing fight for him in the final rounds, but I think he is tough enough and experienced enough to see it through. I'm backing Whyte to get the job done by unanimous decision.
