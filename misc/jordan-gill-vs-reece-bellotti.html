Jordan Gill

10 Round Featherweight Non-Title Fight

Matchroom Fight Camp, Brentwood, Essex - August 1st 2020

Jordan Gill ( 24-1 ) returns to British competition for a bout with fellow former Commonwealth champion Reece Bellotti ( 14-3 ) as part of the first edition of Eddie Hearn's "Matchroom Fight Camp" series. The Cambridgeshire man cut his teeth at world level in 2019 with WBA "International" Featherweight title fights against Emmanuel Dominguez and Mario Enrique Tinoco. However, circumstances dictate that domestic action is currently the order of the day.

Gill is a smooth stylist with a beautiful jab. The 26-year-old uses plenty of movement and likes to box from distance but is very adept at picking the right moment to stand his ground. Gill recorded a 7th round TKO of Ryan Doyle to become Commonwealth champion in October 2018, four months after Doyle had taken the title from Bellotti via 5th round TKO.

The challenger produced a memorable performance in his first 12 round contest. Gill out jabbed Doyle from long range and hurt his opponent with hooks from both the orthodox and southpaw stance when the pair exchanged at close quarters. Doyle found the target frequently with big right hands when he defeated Bellotti but could barely lay a glove on Gill. Whenever it seemed like Doyle might begin to exert pressure Gill used good upper body movement to slip and slide out of range.

Gill was even more impressive when stopping Mexican Emmanuel Dominguez in March 2019. Dominguez had only previously failed to go the distance with world class Super Bantamweight Emanuel Navarrete but was floored three times by Gill before the fight was waved off in the 3rd round. Gill scored with excellent counter right hands and left hooks throughout the contest.

The Doyle and Dominguez fights went like a dream for Gill but it's no exaggeration to say that his bout with Mario Enrique Tinoco in May 2019 turned into something of a nightmare . The powerful little Mexican put Gill under constant pressure from the outset and dropped him in the 3rd, 5th and 8th rounds with body shots. Trainer Gary Coldwell wisely decided to withdraw his charge after the final knock down.

It's tempting to conclude that Tinoco exposed Gill's short comings at world level. This was the first time we'd seen a fighter capable of knocking Gill out of his rhythm. Tinoco roughed Gill up on the inside, slipped the English boxer's jab and peppered him with clubbing blows to the body and head. Gill showed good power against Doyle and Dominguez but wasn't able to make any impression on the battle hardened Mexican. When interviewed in the ring immediately after the contest Gill claimed that he had fallen ill on the day of the fight.

Bellotti is an exciting, if somewhat cavalier, boxer puncher whose vulnerabilities have made for compulsive viewing in the past. His fight with Ryan Doyle in June 2018 was a highly entertaining seesaw affair. Bellotti produced some excellent work and landed eye catching shots but was alarmingly open to right hands. A hard Doyle right hook proved his undoing in the 5th round, although it has to be said that Howard Foster did Bellotti few favours. The referee was extremely hasty in waving the fight off after Bellotti suffered the first knock down of his career.

That defeat seemed to dent Bellotti's confidence somewhat. He appeared more tentative than usual when losing a challenge for Ryan Walsh's British Featherweight title by split decision in December 2018. Bellotti looked to have regained some of his old form when stopping Josue Bendana with a body shot in June 2019 but lost again in his last ring appearance against the Italian Francesco Grandelli.

Boxing away from home in Trento, Italy, Bellotti somewhat predictably found himself on the wrong side of a split decision verdict in favour of the hometown fighter. Bellotti performed very well. He displayed noticeably better head movement and awareness of punches coming his way than he has in the past. The British fighter was unlucky not to have his hand raised after a very close bout against a speedy and awkward opponent.

One thing gamblers may have noted from recent behind-closed-doors events is that it pays to be more wary than usual about betting on a fight to end inside schedule. There is no doubt in my mind that Brad Foster would have pushed harder for a stoppage of James Beech Jr had their British and Commonwealth title bout taken place in front of a passionate Black Country crowd. Certain boxers seem far less inclined to commit to a fight ending onslaught under these quieter, less emotive conditions.

With that in mind, taking into account the defensive improvements Bellotti has shown of late, I'm confident the Hertfordshire man can take Gill 10 rounds. Tinoco showed that Gill can be bullied out of a fight but Bellotti doesn't have the swarming, aggressive, pressure fighting style required to expose that weakness.

Bellotti is the bigger puncher of the two, but Gill should be elusive and skilled enough to make a win inside the distance for his opponent very unlikely. I'm backing Gill to earn a close but unanimous decision after an entertaining contest where both men give a good account of themselves.
