Erickson Lubin

12 Round WBC Light Middleweight Title Eliminator

Mohegan Sun Casino, Connecticut, USA - September 19th

Highly rated 154lb contenders Erickson "The Hammer" Lubin ( 22-1 ) and Terrell Gausha ( 21-1-1 ) will both be hoping to earn a second world title shot when they face each other this weekend.

Lubin and Gausha made their first bids for championship glory on the same night in October 2017. Lubin suffered a shocking 1st round KO defeat to Jermell Charlo in a WBC title challenge. Gausha faired better in his attempt to acquire the WBA belt from Erislandy Lara, but lost by unanimous decision.

Both men have remained undefeated in the time that has passed since those first career setbacks. Lubin has chalked up four consecutive victories since then, two of which came inside schedule. Gausha has been less active. The Cleveland boxer bounced back with a 1st round TKO of Joey Hernandez in December 2018 before a contentious split decision draw with former WBA Light Middleweight champion Austin Trout in May 2019.

Lubin does not seem to have allowed the Charlo debacle to bother him much. In his last ring appearance the Florida southpaw produced arguably the best performance of his 23 fight career when he out pointed the tough Jamaican Nathaniel Gallimore over 10 rounds.

Lubin took a little while to box his way into the contest but once he found his range he was able to score freely with eye catching combinations to body and head. Gallimore never stopped moving forward but had no answer to Lubin's superior speed, timing and mobility.

Four months prior to the October 2019 victory over Gallimore the smooth boxer/puncher proved himself far too powerful and accomplished for Frenchman Zakaria Attou . Lubin softened Attou up with stinging right jabs and straight left hands before unleashing a barrage of hooks to drop his opponent in round four. Attou's corner threw the towel in shortly after the knockdown.

Lubin's left hand can be a real problem for those who face him but Terrell Gausha is a fighter with tremendous experience when it comes to boxing from an orthodox stance against top level southpaws. He acquitted himself well enough against Erislandy Lara , pushing the skilful Cuban back whenever possible and having success with lead right hands and body shots.

The tactics looked to be correct but Gausha was guilty of waiting too long to let his hands go as he advanced. This allowed the crafty champion to consistently steal rounds with well timed bursts of activity. Gausha became a little tentative and hesitant after Lara knocked him down with an uppercut during the 4th round.

The 2012 Olympian seemed to have learned from that experience when he took on Austin Trout . On that occasion he seized the initiative early in proceedings and was a lot more assertive. Trout found himself on the receiving end of a stream of right hands to the head throughout the contest and struggled to find Gausha with his southpaw jab.

Gausha did a much better job of positioning himself to land effective punches against Trout than he had done against Lara, although it should be noted that Trout was past his best and had lost 3 of his last 4 fights. Judge Tony Roberts awarded 9 of the 10 scheduled rounds to Gausha (91-99), but found himself mystifyingly at odds with colleagues John Dixson (96-94 for Trout) and Bill Hunter (95-95). Confirmation of the split decision draw was not well received by those in attendance.

Fresh from that controversy Gausha finds himself up against another dangerous southpaw in Lubin, one who hits harder than Trout or Lara and is considerably younger and fresher at the age of 24. Lubin is certainly the bigger puncher of the two, with 16 of his 22 wins coming via KO/TKO, against just 10 quick wins from 21 for his 33-year-old opponent. Gausha is more of a pressure fighter than Lubin, but he can jab and move well when he chooses to.

Unlike Lubin, Gausha has never failed to hear the final bell in a contest. The older man will certainly take encouragement from the way Jermell Charlo rendered Lubin unconscious inside 3 minutes back in 2017. However, it is debatable how much significance that KO result really has.

In boxing, these things can and do happen. Fighters are often caught cold early in bouts before they've had a chance to adapt to the action. You only have to think back to Lennox Lewis's shock first round defeat to Oliver McCall in 1994 to conclude that it can happen to anyone.

Lubin hasn't been stopped or knocked down in any other fight during his career but Gausha represents the biggest challenge he has faced since the Charlo defeat. None of Lubin's subsequent performances have hinted at residual damage to his confidence or punch resistance, quite the opposite in fact, but Gausha will be keen to determine if that really is the case.

One view the Charlo KO lends credence to is that Lubin can be a slow starter. If Gausha comes out from the opening bell with the same energy and commitment he showed against Trout and is able to back Lubin up consistently, he may be able to establish an early lead.

Ultimately though, I think that Lubin will take over the fight once he gets the measure of Gausha with his southpaw jab. Gausha should be able to land the right hand and will doubtless set a strong pace, but I don't believe he hits hard enough to keep Lubin on the back foot for long. I expect Lubin to start finding the target with hurtful right hooks and straight left hands on a regular basis from the 4th or 5th round onwards.

Gausha may have to dig deep to complete 12 rounds if Lubin starts peppering him with combinations in the latter stages of the bout but he is resilient enough to go the distance. I think Lubin will need to come from behind on the scorecards to win this fight but I expect him to prevail by way of a unanimous decision.
