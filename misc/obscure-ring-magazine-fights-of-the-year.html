For close to a century now The Ring magazine has been selecting bouts for the honour of Fight Of The Year status in their prestigious annual awards. Many of these fights have passed into boxing folklore.

Epic battles like Rocky Graziano vs Tony Zale I and II, Muhammad Ali vs Joe Frazier III and Diego Corrales vs Jose Luis Castillo I are discussed and replayed on a daily basis. This article details five "Fight Of The Year" contests that are less well known, but no less exhilarating than the more celebrated winners.

5

Jose Torres

Jose Torres
vs Eddie Cotton

Convention Center, Las Vegas - August 15th 1966

Jose Torres ( then 37-1-1 ) was defending his WBC and WBA Light Heavyweight world titles for the 2nd time when he faced tough veteran Eddie Cotton ( then 52-20-2 ) in August 1966. The Puerto Rican had captured the titles with a 9th round TKO of Willie Pastrano in March 1965 before defending them for the first time with a comfortable decision victory over Wayne Thornton earlier in the year.

Cotton had lost 5 of his last 10 fights coming into the bout and wasn't considered much of a threat to the champion. As it was, the 40 year old thrilled those in attendance with a courageous and skilful display. The footage below features commentary from Tommy Roberts of Sports Network Incorporated, with contributions from reigning Lightweight champion Carlos Ortiz and former world Heavyweight champion Joe Louis.

Boxing purists will note that Torres was an exponent of the Peek-A-Boo style encouraged by legendary trainer Cus D'Amato and later perfected by his young protege Mike Tyson.

4

Edwin Rosario

Jose Luis Ramirez
vs Edwin Rosario II

Hiram Bithorn Stadium, San Juan, Puerto Rico - November 3rd 1984

Mexican great Jose Luis Ramirez ( then 87-5 ) returned to San Juan in November 1984 hoping to avenge a loss to WBC Lightweight champion Edwin Rosario ( then 24-0 ). The big punching Puerto Rican had claimed the vacant title with a razor thin unanimous decision win over Ramirez in May 1983. The rematch marked the 3rd defence of Rosario's crown.

Many observers considered the unanimous 115-113 scores in favour of Rosario to be controversial. Ramirez blamed his defeat on a slow start in the fight and was determined not to make the same mistake again. Rosario was known as a fast starter but had tired down the stretch during the pair's first meeting. The scene was set for the latest instalment in the great boxing rivalry between Mexico and Puerto Rico.

Commentary is provided by Marv Albert of NBC Sports with assistance from "The Fight Doctor" Ferdie Pacheco. For boxing fans who enjoy literature dedicated to the sweet science, Pacheco's The 12 Greatest Rounds Of Boxing - The Untold Stories is thoroughly recommended.

3

Mahyar Monshipour

Mahyar Monshipour
vs Somsak Sithchatchawal

Palais des Sport Marcel Cerdan, France - March 18th 2006

If Ring magazine journalists had been asked at the outset of 2006 to predict boxers likely to participate in the fight of the year it is difficult to believe any of them would have suggested Mahyar Monshipour ( then 28-2-2 ) or Somsak Sithchatchawal ( then 45-1-1 ). At that point in time Monshipour had only boxed outside of France on one occasion and all of Sithchatchawal's fights had taken place in Thailand.

Sithchatchawal's incredible challenge for the WBA Super Bantamweight title is the only fight he ever had outside of Thailand during his 67 bout career. Something of an unknown quantity coming into the contest, the southpaw had won his last 6 by way of knockout and enjoyed a 4 inch height advantage over Monshipour.

Monshipour had earned the nickname "Little Tyson" with his crowd pleasing style and had become a hugely popular figure in France. The Iranian born fighter became champion after a dramatic 12th round KO of domestic rival Salim Medjkoune in July 2003. Monshipour made 5 successful defences before facing Sithchatchawal.

The bout is fought at a quite astonishing pace in front of a raucous French crowd. If anyone is able to provide me with any information about the Canal Plus commentary team, or any of the French speaking fighters interviewed at ringside during the contest, I would be hugely grateful.

2

Danny Lopez

Danny Lopez
vs Mike Ayala

Convention Center Arena, San Antonio - June 17th 1979

Danny "Little Red" Lopez ( then 40-3 ) seemed odds on to retain his WBC Featherweight title when he travelled to San Antonio to take on home town favourite Mike Ayala ( then 21-1 ) in 1979. The Mexican Irish fighter of American Indian descent was making his 7th defence of the title.

At 21 years of age Ayala was 6 years younger than the vastly more experienced Lopez. The challenger would have to contend with a 3 inch height disadvantage and would need to be wary of the champion's much vaunted power. Lopez is widely regarded as one of the biggest punchers in the history of the 126lb division, with an amazing 39 or his 42 career victories coming inside schedule.

An outstanding performance was required if Ayala was to prove a legitimate threat to Lopez. He did not disappoint. Commentary comes from CBS duo Tim Ryan and Gill Clancy with contributions from Angelo Dundee. Watch out for some amazingly enthusiastic introductions from announcer Bill Merriman and some bizarre refereeing from Carlos Padilla.

1

Robert Quiroga

Robert Quiroga
vs Akeem Anifowoshe

HemisFair Arena, San Antonio, Texas - June 15th 1991

Twelve years after the memorable encounter between Lopez and Ayala, San Antonio boxing fans were again treated to the fight of the year when local hero Robert "Pikin" Quiorga ( then 17-0 ) defended his IBF Super Flyweight title against "Kid" Akeem Anifowoshe ( then 23-0 ). The exciting young champion had earned his title after travelling to Sunderland, England for a unanimous decision win over Colombian Juan Polo Perez in April 1990.

The African challenger turned professional after travelling to the US as part of the Nigerian Olympic team for the 1984 Los Angeles games. Anifowoshe relocated to Las Vegas and proceeded to box his way into title contention on the back of 23 consecutive victories, 18 of which came by KO or TKO. As was the case with Mike Ayala, the hometown favourite Quiroga would be giving up big advantages in height and reach and would need to be at his tenacious best to retain the IBF belt. Commentary on the bout comes from Sam Smith and Robin Welsh.

What follows is one of the most intense and brutal world title fights you are ever likely to see. Unfortunately, the event is now distinguished by tragedy. Anifowoshe suffered a brain injury during the fight which ended his boxing career. He made a full recovery but died 4 years later after returning to Nigeria, in circumstances which remain unclear .

Quiroga's professional career came to an end in 1995, one year after Anifowoshe's death. In August 2004 the former world champion was murdered by a motorcycle club member over a petty dispute. Quiroga's passing was keenly felt in the city of San Antonio, where he is held in great esteem due to his endeavours both in and out of the ring.
