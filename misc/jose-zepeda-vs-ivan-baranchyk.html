Ivan Baranchyk

10 Round Light Welterweight Non-Title Fight

MGM Grand, Las Vegas - 7th July 2020

Top Rank continue to lead the way during a difficult period for the sport. Bob Arum's latest event delivers the most enticing behind closed doors match-up of 2020 so far, as Jose Zepeda (31-2-0 ) takes on Ivan Baranchyk (20-1-0 ) in a meeting of two highly ranked 140lb contenders.

The contest should turn out to be an archetypal "contrast in styles" type of affair. California's Zepeda, the home figter in the contest, is a classy southpaw who likes to stick and move. The Belarusian Baranchyk is a strong, physical boxer who tries to push forward at every opportunity and often overwhelms his opponent with accumulative punishment.

Zepeda has been the victim of considerable misfortune during his career. In July 2015 he was forced to retire after the 2nd round of a WBO Lightweight title fight with Britain's Terry Flanagan due to a dislocated shoulder. Then, in February 2019, Zepeda found himself on the wrong side of a close majority decision verdict in favour of WBC Super Lightweight champion Jose Carlos Ramirez. The man from Long Beach controlled long periods of that bout with his southpaw jab but Ramirez came on strong down the stretch to eke out a slightly controversial points win.

Zepeda bounced back in September 2019 with a comfortable 10 round unanimous decision over Jose Pedraza. The Puerto Rican had only lost to outstanding Lightweight stars Gervonta Davis and Vasiliy Lomachenko in 28 bouts prior to that. Zepeda demonstrated his superb judgement of distance and timing throughout the fight, frequently making his opponent appear tentative and uncoordinated. The 31 year old comprehensively outboxed and outpunched Pedraza from long range in a near faultless performance.

In his last ring appearance Baranchyk recorded an impressive 4th round TKO win over Gabriel Bracero. The tough Puerto Rican had never previously failed to go the distance in his professional career. "The Beast" picked up the WBA Inter-Continental Super Lightweight title with that victory but the belt will not be on the line when he faces Zepeda.

Baranchyk boxes from an orthodox stance but has experience in facing southpaws at world level. He became IBF Super Lightweight champion after getting the best of Sweden's Anthony Yigit in October 2018. Baranchyk gradually broke Yigit down to force a retirement on medical advice after 7 completed rounds of their World Boxing Super Series quarter-final clash. In a gruelling contest, the Belarusian battered Yigit on the inside with clubbing hooks and practically bullied his undefeated opponent out of the fight.

Baranchyk was less successful against a southpaw when losing to Josh Taylor by unanimous decision at the semi-final stage of the competition. The champion set a strong pace but unlike Yigit, Taylor was able to match Baranchyk's work rate and wasn't intimidated by his raw physical aggression. The Scottish fighter had too much speed and movement for Baranchyk, who struggled to find momentum in the fight. Baranchyk landed some good shots in the final rounds as Taylor began to tire, but it was too little, too late.

Both men hit hard enough to hurt their opponents without being considered particularly big punchers. It is difficult to say which of the two has superior power. Neither fighter has been knocked out or stopped during their professional careers. Baranchyk was floored twice in the 6th round of his fight with Taylor and once in the 4th round of a bout with Abel Ramos in 2017. On both occasions he responded well.

Zepeda was dropped in the 4th round of a fight with Carlos Diaz Ramirez in 2018 but got up and knocked Ramirez out in the very next round. Both boxers have shown themselves to be extremely tough and durable up to this point and it would be surprising if the contest did not last the scheduled 10 rounds.

It is not difficult to envisage a scenario where Baranchyk starts fast and disrupts Zepeda's rhythm with body punching and rough, cynical tactics. If he can do this the 27 year old may be able to inflict enough damage early in the fight to nullify his opponent's technical advantages.

However, I believe the bookies are right to make Zepeda a slight 5/4 favourite. Zepeda is the more experienced of the two and has fought higher calibre opposition. Zepeda came very close to defeating Jose Carlos Ramirez despite a 2 inch height and reach disadvantage. The 3½ inch reach advantage he will enjoy over Baranchyk could prove decisive. I believe the American will show good footwork and punch variation to outbox Baranchyk from distance for the first 6-7 rounds of the contest. I expect Zepeda to withstand a late onslaught from the younger man to earn a close but unanimous decision victory.
