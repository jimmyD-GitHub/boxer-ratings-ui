Aleksei Papin

12 Round WBC Cruiserweight Title Eliminator

Pyramide, Kazan, Russia - August 22nd 2020

The hard hitting Aleksei Papin ( 11-1 ) returns to action against fellow Russian Cruiserweight Ruslan Fayfer ( 25-1 ) in a WBC world title eliminator. Papin is expected to get back to winning ways after suffering the first defeat of his professional career to Ilunga Makabu in a fight of the year contender last August.

Papin had ended all 10 of his bouts inside schedule prior to the Makabu fight and betting types will be curious to see if he can start a new KO streak against Fayfer, who is a determined and durable boxer. Papin showed tremendous heart and fighting spirit when losing by split decision to Makabu but lack of experience over the 12 round distance probably cost him against the battle hardened African.

That contest marked the first occasion on which any opponent has extended Papin beyond 8 rounds. The Russian started the bout very well, jabbing and moving fluidly and landing hard shots with both hands. However, it seemed like Papin was guilty of loading up too often during the opening rounds.

Makabu paced the fight better and was more economical with his foot work and punch selection. Papin weakened down the stretch and looked spent during the 12th round. Makabu scored a knockdown in the final minute of the contest to secure victory. Without the extra point the result would have been a split decision draw.

Perhaps Papin felt that he could blow Makabu away in double quick time, just as he had done in November 2017 when dispatching the experienced Light Heavyweight world title challenger Ismayl Sillah in the opening round . Just two months before the Makabu fight Papin had again shown his power to KO the Romanian Alexandru Jur in round three.

Like Papin, Ruslan Fayfer is a strong orthodox fighter who likes to work off his jab. The only blemish on his record so far came in October 2018 when he lost by unanimous decision to the highly rated American Andrew Tabiti. Fayfer gave Tabiti a tough examination in their World Boxing Super Series quarter final but came up short on the cards.

Fayfer is a technically correct boxer with decent power in his right hand. He has never been knocked down as a professional and seems to have a good chin. If Fayfer could box on even terms with Tabiti over 12 rounds he should be capable of providing a stern test for Papin. However, Fayfer is quite methodical and one paced. He does not appear to have the speed or variation required to defeat fighters ranked in the top 10 of the Cruiserweight division.

After losing to Tabiti, Fayfer bounced back with a unanimous decision over Serhiy Radchenko in May 2019 before picking up the lightly regarded "Eurasian Boxing Parliament" Cruiserweight title with another UD win over undefeated compatriot Yury Kashinsky in his last ring appearance.

Papin is a firm favourite at 3/10 (-333.3) to win the contest and it is difficult to argue with those odds. However, I feel that Fayfer is a good bet to last the 12 round distance. The Makabu loss raised one or two questions about how potent Papin's punching power really is at world level. Also, Papin's handlers would probably be happy to see their man win by decision if doing so helps to dispel any doubts about his stamina in a long fight.

It certainly wouldn't come as a surprise if Papin knocks out Fayfer early in the contest but on this occasion, I'm picking him to get the job done by way of a comfortable unanimous decision. The fight might actually turn out to be a lot more competitive and exciting than expected if Fayfer can test Papin's resolve in the 2nd half of the bout.
