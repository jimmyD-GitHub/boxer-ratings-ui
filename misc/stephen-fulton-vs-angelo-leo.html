Angelo Leo

WBO Super Bantamweight World Title Fight

Mohegan Sun Casino, Uncasville, Connecticut - August 1st 2020

Stephen Fulton Jr ( 18-0 ) and Angelo Leo ( 19-0 ) face each other for the vacant WBO Super Bantamweight title in a mouth watering clash between two undefeated young fighters. This is the most significant bout we've seen in the US since boxing made its return behind closed doors.

Both men get their first opportunity to box for a world title after former champion Emmanuel Navarrete dropped the belt in order to become mandatory challenger for the WBO Featherweight title. The WBO have implemented a slightly dubious rule that allows their champions to become mandatory challenger for the title in the division above if they choose to vacate and move up.

The Mexican Navarrete will be fighting at 126lbs for the title vacated by Shakur Stevenson, who presumably will now become mandatory challenger to WBO Super Featherweight champion Jamel Herring. It's like some kind of boxing domino effect.

Leo has shown himself to be a versatile fighter during his early professional career. The Alburqurque man could have used his jab and movement to make his win over Cesar Juarez more comfortable than it was in December 2019. However, Leo was prepared to sit in the pocket right in front of the tough Mexican.

Juarez threw a lot of punches but struggled to land cleanly on Leo, who showed excellent upper body movement and a tight defence under pressure. Leo dropped Juarez heavily with a superb left hook in the 6th round and forced a stoppage in round 11 after hurting Juarez again with a right cross.

Leo was aggressive from the outset in that fight but for the most part he is cautious and methodical. He boxed largely off the back foot when earning a unanimous decision over the cagey southpaw Sharone Carter in August 2018.

That contest marked the 2nd of six unanimous decision victories he recorded over an 18 month period leading up to the Juarez fight. The only bout featuring Leo that did not go the distance during that run was a 1st round KO of Glenn Porras. Leo pole-axed his Filipino opponent with a devastating left hook to the liver inside 2 minutes.

Based on what we have seen so far, Leo creates the impression of being a fighter who generally likes to box at a measured pace from distance, but has the capacity to start hurting opponents at close range if he feels the need to do so. In other words, he is able to "move through the gears" when he wants to assert himself.

It's fair to say that Stephen Fulton has a higher profile than Leo and has faced a higher level of competition since turning professional in 2014. Undefeated records can be deceptive in boxing but Fulton has faced no fewer than six opponents who came into the bout off the back of at least six consecutive wins. Leo has never faced anyone with that kind of recent form.

Fulton is a slick orthodox fighter with a great jab. He often boxes at mid range with his hands held low as a means of luring his opponent forward. Fulton is very adept at countering with left hooks and straight right hands. Those skills were on display in August 2018 when the undefeated Isaac Avelar was knocked out with a body shot during the 6th round.
